abstract: |-
  Dengue is one of the most rapidly spreading mosquito-borne diseases worldwide. Around 1.8 billion of the world's population at risk of dengue lives in member states of the World Health Organization South-East Asia and Western Pacific Regions. (Nathan et.al, 2009) Within these regions, the Philippines ranks one of the highest in reported cases and in deaths compared to its neighboring countries. This is supported by the increase of about 10% in the recorded cases observed during the period of January to May 2015, compared to the same period of 2014. (Department of Health, 2015) To improve the country's current dengue situation, this study was created with the main goal of creating a model using Geographic Information Systems (GIS) and Multiple Linear Regression, specifically Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) and Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR), that predicts the number of dengue cases and forecasts the dengue risk of certain area. The study area was limited to the barangays of Quezon City and the time period is 2010 - 2015. It was determined that the socio-economic factors that affect dengue cases are Informal Settlements and Unused Land (Open Spaces and Very Low Density Residential), while Population is a risk indicator. Environmental factors that significantly affect dengue, on the other hand, are Rainfall and Air Temperature. The final model incorporated these factors and was determined to have an average prediction accuracy of 61.4 % during the rainy season and 33.0% during the rest of the year. It was also determined that the model can forecast dengue risk at 91.2% accuracy, if model outliers were removed.
