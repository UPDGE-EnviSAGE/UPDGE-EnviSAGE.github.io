abstract: |-
  3D information regarding buildings and trees are important for different applications such as urban planning, modelling or visualization etc. The process of extracting such information from both digital surface model (DSM) and digital elevation model (DEM) has always been a challenge, with previous efforts of generating 3D buildings being limited to manual procedures. As such, this paper presents an automated process of generating these 3D objects using geographic information system (GIS). First the normalized digital surface model (nDSM), which contains 3D information of objects above the ground surface, was obtained by subtracting the DEM from the DSM. From there, topographic models were generated, which would be used later for detecting buildings and trees. Detection of buildings and trees were done separately based on their respective characteristics. For building footprints, height, area, and the topographic models were used to separate it from other objects. The extracted footprints were then simplified using orthogonal algorithms for better cartographic quality. Using the simplified footprints, triangular irregular networks (TIN) were created from both DSM and DEM. These TINs were then used to generate the 3D buildings. For trees, height, area, topographic models, and crown detection were used to detect the approximate locations of trees. 3D information was then extracted from the DEM and nDSM. The simplified building footprints were then compared to a manually digitized reference data as an assessment of accuracy. The process was applied on UP Diliman campus. The total processing time took only 112 seconds. The results of the process are promising.
