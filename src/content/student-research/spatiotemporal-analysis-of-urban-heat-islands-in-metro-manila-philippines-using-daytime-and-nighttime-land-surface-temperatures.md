abstract: |-
  Rapid urbanization is a global phenomenon and has uninterruptedly increased over the past few years following the increasing trend in population. Rapid urbanization has also been observed in Metro Manila, being the heart of industrialization and the most populated area in the Philippines. Areas with rapid industrialization typically experience the Urban Heat Island phenomenon, which causes higher air and land surface temperatures that affect the daily lives of Filipinos in their routine and lifestyle. This research aims to examine the trend of the daytime and nighttime land surface temperatures in Metro Manila, the extent of the Urban Heat Island phenomenon in the area and determine the biggest contributors in the rise of such phenomenon in order to establish a reliable, cost-free method and aid further research on the topics in terms of Urban Planning and the general public's well-being. Using the free and accessible Landsat 8 satellite imagery, the Land Surface Temperature (LST) and Surface Urban Heat Island Intensity (SUHII) layers are generated and compared from years 2014 to 2021. It was observed, through the SUHII maps generated, that an increasing trend in the intensity can be observed at nighttime and can be attributed to certain materials used in the construction of infrastructures and facilities for anthropogenic convenience.
