abstract: |-
  Infrastructures in the Philippines which include the drainage pipe system, are often damaged due to these hazardous events which in turn cause a lot of detrimental effects to the people and the environment such as flooding, sewage contamination, and other adverse health effects. In addition to that, problematic pipes are difficult to check as they are located underground. With the use of ground penetrating radar (GPR) technology, this study aims to determine the conditions underlying the pipes through the analysis of the GPR signal transmitted into the pipe. GPR surveys are conducted in Adelfa Street in Quezon City which is flood prone due to clogged pipes upon inspection. Different set-ups depicting varied conditions of a drainage pipe using an experimental test facility are carried out in determining drainage pipe hyperbolic responses. Results from the GPR surveys in the study area are compared to the outcomes of the experimental setup, and they show that the drainage pipes in Adelfa Street can be classified into either partially or completely filled with water. K-values from the decaying exponential curve wave envelope served as the basis in classifying the conditions of drainage pipes of the study area. K-values, within the range of 0.305 to 0.534 obtained from the exponential curve functions of two experimental setups, describe a pipe that is partially filled with water, while less than 0.305 is classified as a pipe completely filled with water, and greater than 0.534 is unclassified. Construction and drainage companies will benefit by having an efficient work with the aid of GPR technology and to locate the inside pipe conditions.
