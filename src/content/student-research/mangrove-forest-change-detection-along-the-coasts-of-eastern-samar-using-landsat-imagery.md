abstract: |-
  Mangroves are groups of salt-tolerant trees and shrub species that grow along intertidal coastlines at tropical and subtropical latitudes. Mangrove forests play significant roles in protecting coastal areas from erosion, reducing the impact of storm surges and tsunamis, maintaining water quality by trapping sediments and pollutants, and serving as habitat and source of food to a large population of marine species. However, the total mangrove area in the Philippines has come to diminish due to various anthropogenic activities such as coastal and aquaculture development, land use conversion, resource extraction, pollution and siltation, and natural phenomenon such as sea-level rise associated with climate change and increased sedimentation along coastlines. This study aims to investigate the changes of mangrove forests along the coast of Eastern Samar (mainly located in three municipalities: Salcedo, Mercedes, and Guian) using Landsat imageries during the years 2001, 2013, and 2015. The remotely sensed data was processed through four (4) main steps: (1) data pre-processing including geometric and atmospheric corrections, and cloud, cloud shadow, and high elevation masking (2) image classification using Maximum Likelihood supervised classification algorithm; (3) accuracy assessment for the classification results, and (4) change detection analysis. Classification for years 2001, 2013 and 2015 yielded an overall accuracy of 93.33%, 94.17% and 93.00%, respectively. The Kappa coefficient is 0.9167, 0.9271, and 0.9125, respectively. It was determined that from 2001 to 2015, a total of 622.71 hectares of mangrove were lost. 229.32 hectares were lost from 2001 to 2013 and 393.93 hectares from 2013 to 2015.
