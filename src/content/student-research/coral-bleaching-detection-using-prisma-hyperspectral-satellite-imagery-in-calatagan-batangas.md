abstract: |-
  Coral bleaching is an occurrence where corals experience stress due to various reasons, including rising ocean temperatures and pollution caused by coastal expansion. Several studies on bleaching detection utilize multispectral imagery, due to data availability. In this study, the usage of hyperspectral data imagery, specifically, PRISMA, was assessed for coral bleaching detection in Calatagan, Batangas, and sea surface temperature (SST) for confirming bleaching occurrence. In-situ measurements were gathered for spectral data of live and bleached corals, and a qualitative data interview with a coral bleach patroller was conducted to gain further information about bleaching details in the area. Sentinel-2 L2A was also used for comparison during classification. The satellite images were noise reduced, undergone water column correction, masked, and georeferenced. Spectral Angle Mapper (SAM) was utilized for image classification, while using field data and data from related studies for spectral library. A total of 27.12% relative to the coral reef area was classified as bleached corals. T-test statistics with 95% confidence level was done to analyze the separability of live and bleached corals, which exhibited that only two (2) bands in the green region found to be not separable. Box plot was also generated to see the trend of the bands, showing that bleached corals have generally lower reflectance values than live corals for RGB region, as compared to Sentinel, with bleached having higher values, which can imply that spatial resolution can affect the spectral response of different features. Results were validated using ground truth data obtaining a 60.6% user's accuracy for bleached corals, and 63.4% overall accuracy. In conclusion, hyperspectral satellite imagery can be utilized in coral bleaching detection, despite its limitations. Additional parameters such as depth variables and diverse sample points are recommended for the betterment of coral bleaching detection using PRISMA.
