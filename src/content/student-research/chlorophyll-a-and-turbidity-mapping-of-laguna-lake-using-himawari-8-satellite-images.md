abstract: |-
  The Laguna Lake is the largest inland body of water in the Philippines with various uses, fisheries as the most dominant one, making water quality monitoring necessary and important. Traditional water quality assessment methods are expensive and arduous as it requires intensive fieldwork in order to collect in-situ data from the lake. Remote sensing provides a modern solution through the utilization and analysis of satellite images. However, the current and common satellites used only provide analysis at most twice a month. Himawari-8 satellite has a temporal resolution of one image taken every 10 minutes, providing a way to analyze multiple images within a day resulting in a more accurate and more frequent water quality analysis. Parameters estimated are the Chlorophyll-a (CHL-a), which indicates the number of algae living in the water, and Turbidity (TUR), which is an indication of the amount of particles suspended in the water and how much light can get through. After processing the Himawari-8 images, two types of analysis were done: (1) analysis using a single image with the in-situ data, and; (2) multiple images, which corresponds to the specific time the in-situ data was observed. The researchers were able to generate linear regression models using single and multiple images to estimate the chlorophyll-a and turbidity for the dates March 25, and April 6, 2019. The adjusted R² of the best models for March 25 using single image are 0.293 and 0.225 for CHL-a and TUR respectively, while for April 6 using single image, the adjusted R² are 0.712 and 0.759 for CHL-a and TUR respectively. On the other hand, for March 25 using multiple images, the adjusted R² values are 0.529 for CHL-a and 0.738 for TUR, and for April 6 using multiple images, 0.547 for CHL-a and 0.818 for TUR. Resulting linear regression models and maps using multiple images showed more consistent and better water quality parameter estimates than using single image.
