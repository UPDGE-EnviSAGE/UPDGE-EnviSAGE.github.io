abstract: |-
  There is a lack of efficient accounting of coconut cover in the country that utilizes the current technological innovations. Through the years, manual field measurement has been the only method to quantify coconut cover in the Philippines, which are both tedious and laborious, while coconut cover estimates can be obtained by employing Remote Sensing techniques on satellite images. This study aims to address this issue and provide a rapid means of estimating coconut fractional cover by using Mixture Tuned Matched Filtering (MTMF) on a Landsat 8 image. A subset image covering Mati City, Davao Oriental with an area of 646 hectares was used as the study area. MTMF is a linear unmixing method employed on mixed pixels, which suppresses background noise and estimates the sub-pixel abundance of a single target material. (Mitchell & Glenn, 2009) This method involves the atmospheric correction of the Landsat image using FLASH (Fast Line-of-sight Atmospheric Analysis of Hypercubes), the output of which is to be used in the Minimum Noise Fraction (MNF) Transform to reduce the computational requirements and segregate noise in the background spectra of the image. Highly pure pixels, as shown from the high-resolution classified coconut cover map derived from LiDAR, were selected as endmembers. Multiple Linear Regression of the resulting Matched Filtering (MF) Score and Infeasibility as independent variables against the Fractional Cover (dependent variable) from the validation data was employed. Results show that MTMF estimates did not fully estimate the fractional abundance of coconut cover based on the regression equations generated and the low R-squared values. The models that were only significant are for pixels with fractional coverage of 40-50%, 70-80%, 80-90%, and 90-100%. These results are probably influenced by the heterogeneous differences in spatial distribution patterns at various percentage covers of coconut throughout the study area.
