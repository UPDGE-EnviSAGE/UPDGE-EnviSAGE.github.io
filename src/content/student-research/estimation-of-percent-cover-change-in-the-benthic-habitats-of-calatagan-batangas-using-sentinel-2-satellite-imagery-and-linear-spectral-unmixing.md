abstract: |-
  The Verde Island Passage, known for its diverse marine ecosystem, is facing increasing threats from economic factors and human activities. In the municipality of Calatagan, Batangas, which borders the Verde Island Passage, there is a lack of comprehensive documentation for coral reefs and seagrass meadows. Previous studies have utilized various classification algorithms to analyze benthic habitat features, but misclassifications often occur due to the complex nature of these habitats. To address this issue, the linear spectral unmixing technique was employed in this study to classify benthic habitats in the coastal areas of Calatagan, Batangas. The primary objective of the study was to estimate the percentage change in habitat coverage between 2016 and 2022. Remote sensing techniques were applied using Sentinel-2 Level 1C imagery, GEBCO bathymetry data, and spectral signatures of corals and seagrass. The study encompassed six procedures: field data collection, image preprocessing, deepwater masking, linear spectral unmixing, accuracy assessment, and estimation of percentage cover change. Validation was conducted using drone and Planetscope images, with R-squared and RMSE serving as accuracy metrics. The results revealed a significant decline in coral coverage by 20.4%, while seagrass exhibited a positive change of 10.2%. Additionally, the fractional abundance maps for corals and seagrass were generated.
