abstract: |-
  Remote sensing and GIS have are addressing the need for image processing from a variety of different sensors and datasets to map, detect and monitor forest and vegetation cover. Vegetation cover change using remote sensing and GIS can be helpful in predicting the natural calamities to provide humanitarian aid, damage assessment and furthermore to develop new protection and conservation strategies. The study aims to utilize change detection techniques and spatial analysis in mapping and describing vegetation cover change in the Samar Island, Philippines which is currently experiencing vegetation decrease due to natural disasters and anthropological activities. Spatial-temporal analysis will be employed using Landsat Images of various years. Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and Tasseled Cap Transformation (ICT) were the detection techniques used in the spatial-temporal analysis of Landsat Images of Samar Island, Philippines of the years 1994 and 2004. As of 2004, Samar Island is experiencing approximately 39.27% and 38.74% on vegetation degradation based on hotspot analysis using NDVI and TCT Greenness component respectively.
