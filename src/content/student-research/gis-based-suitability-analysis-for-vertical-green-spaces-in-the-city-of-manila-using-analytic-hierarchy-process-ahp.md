abstract: |-
  Urban green spaces (UGS) are crucial elements of the urban structure that improve the quality of life and behaviour of the residents. Because of the rapid population growth and urbanization in the City of Manila, there has been a need for developing UGS. Locating areas where the benefits of these UGS can be maximized is a significant step in the urban development of the city. This study presents a GIS-based suitability analysis for evaluating suitable sites for urban green space development using analytic hierarchy process (AHP). The study focuses only on vertical gardens due to the dense urban environment of the city. The factors considered for the study are population density (21.07%), land surface temperature (LST) (19.64%), air quality (27.32%), and land cover (31.97%). The computed criteria weights were obtained from consulting 17 participants, including local stakeholders and experts from government agencies, industry, and academia sectors. The aggregated AHP results show a consistency ratio (CR) of 4.9%. The generated suitability map shows that the majority of the areas in Manila are classified as lower suitability which are located at the northern and southern parts of the city with an area of 11.64 sq. km. or 31.75% of the total area. Most higher and high level areas of suitability are located at the central and eastern parts of the city with an area of 5.83 sq. km. and 5.77 sq. km., respectively. Furthermore, the majority of the areas under these levels are identified to be in the High Density Residential/Mixed-Use Zones. The findings of this study can be utilized for the urban green space development of the city. The tools and methodology used can be applied in other cities within the Philippines with modifications depending on the objectives and conditions of the locality.
