abstract: |-
  This study focuses on Tangalan Bay, renowned for its rich marine ecosystems, including seagrass, macroalgae, corals, and various marine species. Satellite images from PlanetScope, Sentinel-2, Landsat-9, KOMPSAT, and SPOT 6-7 were analyzed alongside ground truth data from previous fieldwork. Using Google Earth Engine (GEE), three classification levels were defined: Level 1 for mapping five benthic classes, Level 2 for assessing coral health, and Level 3 for estimating seagrass percent cover. Supervised classification techniques included Pixel-Based and Object-Based Image Analysis with Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Classification And Regression Tree algorithms. Hyperparameter tuning and cross-validation ensured balanced class distribution. The results showed that for PlanetScope, Sentinel-2, Landsat-9, KOMPSAT, and SPOT, the best combination for Level 1 was OBIA with Random Forest, while PBIA with SVM was optimal for Level 2. The results showed that Object-Based Image Analysis (OBIA) performed better for broad habitat mapping, while Pixel-Based Image Analysis (PBIA) excelled in distinguishing between dead and live corals. Spectral unmixing for seagrass cover across all images yielded low accuracy, indicating the need for improved methodologies. These insights underscore the importance of choosing appropriate classification methods for specific study goals, enhancing environmental monitoring and conservation efforts.
