abstract: |-
  Freshwater bodies such as Taal Lake are vital for communities' resources on fresh drinking water and livelihood. Pollutants in these types of water provide long-term harmful environmental effects for the lake's ecosystem and the residents it houses. This research aimed to examine the current situation of Taal lake by estimating chlorophyll-a (chl-a) and turbidity values using Sentinel-2 and PRISMA imagery through the implementation of Water Colour Simulator (WASI) and water quality indices. The spatial analysis of the satellite image-derived values revealed distinct variations in chlorophyll- and turbidity concentrations across Taal Lake. Both satellite systems consistently depicted higher chlorophyll-a concentrations (1.3-5.9 μg/L for Sentinel-2; 1.4-5.4 μg/L for PRISMA) in the western regions and lower concentrations (1.7-3.4 μg/L for Sentinel-2, 1.9-3.3 μg/L for PRISMA) in the southern areas. Similarly, turbidity concentrations (0.15-0.59 mg/L for Sentinel-2; 0.13-0.51 mg/L for PRISMA) were observed in the eastern and northeastern portions of the lake. Water quality indices (NDTI & NDCI) also produced similar results with the WASI-produced maps. The NDCI maps indicate high chl-a values in the northwestern portion of the lake, while the NDTI maps exhibited higher turbidity concentrations in the northwestern, northern, and eastern regions. These patterns aligned with the field data, indicating the efficiency of Sentinel-2 and PRISMA in capturing and characterizing the spatial variability of water quality parameters. The comparative analysis of PRISMA hyperspectral and Sentinel-2 multispectral images for chlorophyll-a and turbidity mapping in Taal Lake demonstrated the potential of both satellite systems in providing valuable insights into the spatial distribution of water quality parameters. While both systems showed similar spatial patterns for the correlation between chlorophyll-a and turbidity, the discrepancies observed in the scatter plots and inversion failures in PRISMA highlight the need for further research and refinement in the utilization of PRISMA data with the WASI model. Overall, these findings emphasize the importance of considering temporal and spatial factors when analyzing water quality parameters using remote sensing data.
