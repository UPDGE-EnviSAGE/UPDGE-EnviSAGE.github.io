abstract: |-
  Boracay Island, renowned for its pristine beaches and global recognition, faced challenges arising from haphazard practices on infrastructural management, improper waste management, and environmental degradation. A rehabilitation program was initiated following a temporary island closure, involving the demolition of coastal structures and the implementation of improved sewage systems. Along with these efforts are the visible enhancements to water quality, including reduced levels of fecal coliform and the absence of algal blooms. Recognizing the link between water quality and land cover change, this study investigates the impact of LC on water quality parameters (chl-a and TSM), specifically in the Island of Boracay. By leveraging Sentinel-2 MSI data, field measurements, and MODIS data, the study analyzes LC patterns across three key periods: pre-rehabilitation (2017), rehabilitation (2020), and post-rehabilitation (2022) of Boracay Island. Quarterly assessments of chl-a and TSM concentrations are conducted to evaluate water quality. The findings reveal significant variations in chl-a and TSM with significance values of 0.043 and 0.014, respectively, within zone 1 of Boracay Island. Post-hoc test revealed the variations in chl-a concentrations between 2017 and subsequent years (2020, p = 0.038; 2021, p = 0.036 ; and 2022, p = 0.039) as well as between 2019 and subsequent years (2020, p = 0.043; 2021, p = 0.041; and 2022, p = 0.044); and in TSM concentrations between 2017 and subsequent years (2020, p = 0.006; 2021, p = 0.008; and 2022, p = 0.023) as well as between 2019 and subsequent years (2020, p = 0.014; 2021, p = 0.019; and 2022, p = 0.049). However, no significant correlation is observed between LC and water quality parameters. To further advance the field, future research is recommended to refine LC classification through high-resolution satellite imagery, explore alternative methods for LC extraction, consider extended timeframes, and investigate additional water quality parameters. This study contributes valuable insights into the environmental transformations of Boracay Island and offers guidance for sustainable coastal management in similar contexts.
