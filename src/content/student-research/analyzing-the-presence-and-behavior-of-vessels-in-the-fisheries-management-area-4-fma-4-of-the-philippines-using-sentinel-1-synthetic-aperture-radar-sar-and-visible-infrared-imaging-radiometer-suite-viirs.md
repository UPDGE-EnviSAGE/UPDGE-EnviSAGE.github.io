abstract: |-
  The Fisheries Management Area 4 (FMA-4) in the Philippines, despite its abundant marine resources, grapples with persistent maritime crimes and illegal fishing, exposing the limitations of current vessel monitoring systems. To address this challenge, this study harnessed the potential of Sentinel-1 SAR and VIIRS satellite imagery to detect vessel presence and analyze its behavior within FMA-4 from September 2022 to August 2023. A rigorous methodology involving preprocessing, correction, filtering, classification, and cross-matching of satellite data against the Automatic Identification System (AIS) yielded a robust database of identified vessels. The study successfully detected a wide range of vessels, with an 80.65% validation rate against AIS data, demonstrating the effectiveness of satellite imagery in maritime domain awareness. Notably, the study also identified vessels not registered in AIS, revealing potential unregulated maritime activity. Results also revealed concentrated vessel activity between Sulu and Tawi-Tawi, primarily linked to rich fishing grounds and the presence of numerous unregistered vessels, suggesting potential illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing. The prevalence of foreign-flagged vessels, particularly those from China, further emphasizes the need for enhanced governance and international cooperation to ensure sustainable resource management and safeguard sovereignty. The findings of this study have significant implications for various stakeholders, including government agencies, advocacy organizations, and the fishing industry. By demonstrating the power of satellite-based monitoring in enhancing maritime domain awareness and identifying potential threats, this research contributes to a more secure and accountable maritime environment, ultimately promoting sustainable practices and protecting valuable marine resources in FMA-4.
