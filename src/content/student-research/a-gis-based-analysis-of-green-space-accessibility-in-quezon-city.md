abstract: |-
  Metro Manila, being a concrete jungle, has a lack of greenspaces that are accessible to everyone, making it difficult for its residents to reap the benefits of greenspaces. There may be enough greenspaces for the whole of Quezon City, but there is an evident case of environmental injustice because of how greenspaces are spatially distributed in it. Multiple researches regarding greenspace accessibility have been done in other countries using network analysis as a tool for evaluation; however, there has not been any research done regarding greenspace accessibility in Quezon City. With this, the main goal of this research is to assess the accessibility and availability of greenspaces in the city using network analysis. Data used were all bound in Quezon City such as its tree cover, land classification, list of parks from Quezon City Parks Development and Administration Department, road network, and its administrative boundaries. Greenspace accessibility in Quezon City is tested through the Accessible Natural Greenspace Standard (ANGSt). Greenspaces were identified through the use of land classification and tree cover. Land classifications that were used were forests, parks, meadows, and recreation grounds. For tree cover, the percentage that was used was 30% based on the 3-30-300 rule. Three types of greenspaces were classified according to their sizes [< 2 hectare greenspaces (Type 1 Greenspace), 2 ≤ X < 20 hectare greenspaces (Type 2 Greenspace) and ≥ 20 hectare greenspaces (Type 3 Greenspace)]. Results confirmed that not all barangays have access to all types of greenspaces and others do not have access at all. The findings of this study underscore a diverse landscape of greenspace accessibility across barangays, revealing a notable lack of uniformity in the provision of these vital urban amenities. Notably, the prevalence of greenspaces varies significantly, with Type 1 Greenspaces having the most number being in 51 barangays, followed by Type 2 Greenspaces in 43 barangays and Type 3 Greenspaces in 19 barangays. This hierarchical distribution influences the accessibility of greenspaces within barangays, with Type 1 Greenspaces emerging as the most accessible, closely followed by Type 2 and Type 3 Greenspaces. Interestingly, the area coverage analysis unveils a contrasting pattern, with Type 3 Greenspaces exhibiting the highest spatial extent in terms of area, succeeded by Type 1 and Type 2 Greenspaces. Another key revelation is the inadequacy of greenspaces per person in several barangays, indicating potential disparities in greenspace provisions. While a general trend associates higher tree cover with increased greenspace availability, this relationship is not consistently observed across all barangays. These nuanced findings emphasize the need for targeted urban planning strategies to address disparities and ensure a more equitable and sustainable greenspace environment, considering both the prevalence and accessibility of different greenspace types within the urban landscape.
