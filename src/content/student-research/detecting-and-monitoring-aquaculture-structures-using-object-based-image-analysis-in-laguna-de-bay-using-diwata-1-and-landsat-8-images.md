abstract: |-
  This research aims to address one of the problems of Laguna Lake's authorities with regards to the 10% allotment for aquaculture purposes. The factors considered were that the area is too tedious to monitor using traditional field methods, and that only remotely sensed satellite images were the viable source of information. To achieve this, the researchers analyzed satellite images from Diwata-1 and Landsat 8. The researchers wanted to make use of Diwata-1 because it is the Philippine's first microsatellite which was just launched in 2016. In using Diwata-1 and Landsat 8 satellite images to detect and monitor aquaculture structures, the researchers hope to create a method that could provide authorities with a cost-effective means of mapping and monitoring the Largest Lake in the Philippines despite being limited to the West Bay of Laguna Lake which is the area common to the satellites images from both Diwata-1 and Landsat 8. The methodology that the researchers developed starts by performing radiometric corrections on the satellite images in order to enhance the spectral data, however applying this correction to the Diwata images worsened the result of segmentation, so the researchers opted not to apply radiometric calibrations for the Diwata image in order to obtain better segments for object-based image analysis. Before any further processing, the Diwata images also had to be geometrically corrected because the downloaded bands had a slight offset from one another. Different methods were used on Diwata-1 and Landsat 8 images due to the differences in downloadable information among other reasons. The researchers then applied image filtering and enhancement methods to make the aquaculture structures more visible. The enhanced images were then processed through Cognition, treating these features as objects. By doing this, they can be classified and isolated from the images and provide an output at the end of this research showing maps of the lake with the fish pens outlined. In addition, the structures were also manually digitized and counted to have a means of comparison to the software generated count to obtain an accuracy measurement. The results produced by the study reveals that performing object-based image analysis to classify satellite images of Laguna Lake can be used as a method of detecting and monitoring aquaculture structures.
