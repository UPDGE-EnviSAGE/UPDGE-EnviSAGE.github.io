abstract: |-
  In the Philippines, Sargassum spp., a genus of brown macroalgae, plays an important role in the ecological and socio-economic aspects of different communities. It serves as an important source of alginates, habitat for myriad lifeforms, and contributes to the livelihood of fisher folk in coastal communities. Its high commercial value led to overharvesting, posing threats to the balance of the marine ecosystem. To date, there is a limited number of studies focusing on the distribution of the brown macroalgae in the country. To establish the present condition of Sargassum spp. in the Philippines, the National Fisheries Research and Development Institute (NFRDI) is currently researching on the management and assessment of its distribution. In line with the NFRDI's objective, this study aims to present the percent cover and spatial distribution of Sargassum spp. around the southern coastal areas of Busuanga, Philippines through remote sensing applications of Sentinel-2A Satellite and DII Phantom 3 UAV imagery. To distinguish Sargassum spp. from other classes, image interpretation and spectral analysis were performed. Three supervised classification methods: Maximum Likelihood, Linear Support Vector Machine, and Radial Basis Function Support Vector Machine were used in the image processing. Results show that Maximum Likelihood produced the best results for both datasets. The Maximum Likelihood classifier yielded the highest overall accuracy rates with 95.07% (kappa coefficient, κ = 0.94) and 80.79% (kappa coefficient, κ = 0.76) for the UAV and Sentinel-2A image, respectively.
