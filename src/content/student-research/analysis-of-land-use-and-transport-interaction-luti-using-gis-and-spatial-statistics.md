abstract: |-
  Traffic is an ever-recurring problem in the Philippines. Quezon City, the most populous city in the Philippines, vastly struggle with this problem due to increasing number of population and facilities (mixed land use) and decreasing quality of accessibility (transport). This research aims to investigate the relationship between the trip mode choice of people in Districts 1, 3, and 4 of Quezon City to land use form, demographic and socioeconomic variables. For each of the trip mode choices, the number of trips from a zone to all the other zones was obtained. This was used as the dependent variable in the research study. Areas of the Land use form were weighted by distance to give the attractiveness, of all areas of that particular type of land use in the study area from each zone. Traffic data was also aggregated to each of the zones. Demographic and socioeconomic variables such as population, average household income and car ownership were also included in the regression analysis. The research study was able to identify land use forms and trip purposes that have significant impact on people's trip mode choice using GIS and Spatial Statistics Demographic, socioeconomic and traffic data however proved to have less significant effect to trip mode choice as compared to land used form and trip purposes. Geographically weighted regression (GWR), and spatial lag regression (SLR) were used to improve model performance of the ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, as these are able to model the spatial non-stationarity and dependency in the data, unlike a global regression like OLS. A significant improvement of the motorcycle trip generation OLS model was observed in the decrease of the Akaike Information Criterion from 629.370 to 623.709 and an increase in the adiusted R² from 0.724 to 0.795. Spatial lag regression on the other hand was applied to correct the significant clustering as observed in the spatial autocorrelation test of the Bus trip generation OLS model. The model was improved through the removal of spatial dependencies by adding spatial weights in performing the spatial lag regression. A decrease in the Akaike Information Criterion from 661.390 to 655.615 was observed. Results of the study can be utilized as a basis on proper land use allocation for more sustainable urban mobility.
