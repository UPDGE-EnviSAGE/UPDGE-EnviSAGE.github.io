abstract: |-
  Effective conservation and management of water bodies are backed by accurate and timely water quality monitoring. Employment of remote sensing-based approaches with machine learning opens opportunities for near real-time monitoring, especially with high temporal resolution data. This study aims to estimate chl-a and SPM concentrations of Laguna Lake in near real-time by producing optimized seasonal models using Himawari-8 images and LightGBM machine learning algorithm. Among the 25 input features, it was found that the Blue/Green band ratio contributed most to the predictions of all models. The dominance of Red and NIR bands for SPM estimation was also evident in the feature importance scoring. The optimized models had fairly acceptable R2 (dry=0.39 to 0.57, wet=0.39 to 0.48), and low RMSE (dry=3.85 to 15.89, wet=2.84 to 18.55), MAE (dry=2.55 to 10.41, wet=1.74 to 12.73) and MAPE (dry=0.38 to 2.77, wet=0.58 to 4.22) upon model evaluation. Moreover, the model-generated maps captured the spatial and temporal variability of chl-a and SPM concentrations in the lake. Validation of the models using in situ measurements showed that there was a large difference between the predicted values and field survey values. This is mainly because of the disagreement in values between the training and in situ data used in the study.
