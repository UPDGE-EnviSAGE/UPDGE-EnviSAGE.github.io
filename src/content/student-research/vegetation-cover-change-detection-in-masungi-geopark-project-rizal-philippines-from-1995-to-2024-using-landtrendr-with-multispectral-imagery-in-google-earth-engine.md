abstract: |-
  The Masungi landscape in Rizal, Philippines, has faced persistent ecological threats from quarrying, road construction, and other human activities, necessitating long-term, accurate monitoring of forest changes to support sustainable conservation strategies (Dumaliang, 2018; Cabrera, 2023). Hence, change in the Masungi landscape (1995–2024) was assessed using the LandTrendr algorithm applied to harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 NDVI composites. The study aimed to quantify gradual gain and abrupt loss, characterize spatiotemporal patterns, and evaluate algorithm performance in detecting ecologically meaningful change. Annual medoid mosaics were segmented to extract year, magnitude, and duration of change per pixel. Spatial patterns were interpreted using RGB composites, NDVI trajectories, and validated with high-resolution imagery. Results showed that forest gain dominated, with 328.23 hectares identified as regrowth, especially along the Marcos Highway corridor. In contrast, abrupt vegetation loss was scattered, totaling 80.19 hectares. Most loss events occurred within the NDVI value of 0.2-0.3 which were concentrated near roads, ridgelines, and quarry areas. Temporal peaks of abrupt loss were detected in 1996 and 2024. Accuracy assessment reported an overall accuracy of 83.33% and a Kappa coefficient of 0.75, reflecting reliable detection of vegetation cover dynamics. These findings highlight LandTrendr’s effectiveness in capturing both gradual and abrupt vegetation trends, underscore the success of conservation strategies, and identify recurring disturbance zones requiring sustained management.
