abstract: |-
  Inconsistencies in Philippine air quality reports have risen over the past years due to the limited number of operational air quality monitoring stations in the country. This study shows the feasibility of using satellite images, specifically the Landsat 8 Coastal/Aerosol band, and remote sensing and GIS techniques for determining the condition of air quality in urban areas by obtaining the aerosol optical thickness (AOT) as an alternative for the costly and time-consuming ground-based measurements using different sensors. AOT maps of Metro Manila were produced using the Path Radiance equation. AOT values were determined from Landsat 8 images acquired on 2015 to 2018 with 0% to 50% cloud cover for Metro Manila using the Raster Calculation method in ArcGIS. Temporal and spatial variations in AOT are obtained through Optimized Hotspot Analysis in GIS. The image-derived AOT were validated using the AERONET (CE318-T Multiband Sun-Sky-Lunar Photometer sensor) v3 Level 1.5 total AOT 440 values from Manila Observatory. The results show a seasonal trend in the AOT values, where they are higher during the dry season (November to April), ranging from 0.0247 to 0.0743 as compared to the values for the wet season (May to October), ranging from 0.0242 to 0.0342. The results also indicate that AOT values are inversely proportional to the amount of precipitation during the years 2015 and 2016. From the optimized hotspot analysis, the study shows that the higher AOT values cluster around major roads, business districts, and industrial areas. The results from this study can be used to assess the air quality condition of an area, and the risks posed by existing air pollutants, and to aid in urban planning and in the suitability analysis for additional air quality monitoring stations in the country.
