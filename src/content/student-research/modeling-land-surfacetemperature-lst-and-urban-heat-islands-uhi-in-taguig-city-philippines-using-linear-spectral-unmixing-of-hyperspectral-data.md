abstract: |-
  As one of the fastest developing countries in the world, urbanization in the Philippines occurs at a rapid pace. As this growth continues, newly urbanized areas begin to reshape our country's environment and alter its natural material composition. The climatic impact that impervious materials common to urbanized areas have is one that has been well established; however, the contributions of specific materials are not as well characterized. This paper addresses the limitations that exist in general urban heat island analyses by using linear spectral unmixing (LSU) of a hyperspectral image in tandem with land surface temperature (LST) data to establish the relationship between material abundance and LST, express the relationship between surface material abundance alongside LST using correlation analysis, and providing information on how materials contribute to surface temperature. These were accomplished by the simultaneous processing of Landsat-9's thermal products alongside PRISMA's hyperspectral images; with Landsat-9 providing the necessary temperature data while PRISMA is the basis for material fraction maps. PRISMA's results were then validated by way of comparison to pixel-based classification of a 3-meter resolution PlanetScope image resulting in R^2 values of 0.8420 and 0.9007 for vegetation and built-up areas, respectively. Afterwards, LST values and abundance fractions were overlaid and processed to derive correlation values of LST to vegetation (r = -0.7808), asphalt (r = 0.4868), galvanized iron sheets (r = 0.7260), water (r = -0.3376), and bare soil (r = -0.2588). Likewise, correlation values with UTFVI produced values of UTFVI to vegetation (r = -0.7794), asphalt (r = 0.4836), galvanized iron sheets (r = 0.7229), water (r = -0.3419), and bare soil (r = -0.2555). Multiple Linear Regression also provided coefficients for vegetation (-2.0999), asphalt (4.9656) and GI Sheets (6.0401), with a multiple R of 0.8049, R^2 of 0.6478, and RMSE of 1.4280 in respect to LST. Whereas UTFVI reported coefficient values for vegetation (-0.0068), asphalt (0.0156), and GI Sheets (0.0189), with a multiple R of 0.8025, an R^2 of 0.6440, and an RMSE of 0.0806. From these results, it can be concluded that of the endmembers, the presence of vegetation produced a strong cooling effect on surface temperatures, whereas both watered and barren soil areas have little to no effect on surface temperatures. In regards to impervious materials, asphalt is shown to have a moderate effect on the increase of temperature, while galvanized iron sheets have the highest effect on temperature increase.
