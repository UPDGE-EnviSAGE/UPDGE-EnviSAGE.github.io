abstract: |-
  Assessments of road surface conditions nowadays are still generally manual in nature. The Detailed Policy Guidelines On The Maintenance Of National Roads And Bridges of the DPWH, for instance, details the procedure of monitoring road surface condition in the Philippines. Basing mainly on the judgement of the inspectors, the quantity and severity of the road defects are recorded. Integration of technology in the method may prove to increase efficiency, reduce risk, avoid intrusion, and prompt road maintenance. The proposed method of Road Surface Modelling Using Terrestrial Vehicle-Mounted Action Camera addresses the need for an alternative methodology of road surface condition monitoring and also the limitation of UAV-based methods in areas under canopy. In this research, a methodology using images extracted from GoPro videos to model road surface is designed and validated with ground truth values and terrestrial laser scanner (TLS) data. Parameters such as vehicle speed, camera height and image extraction rate were fixed while camera placement (center and side) on the vehicle and dataset combinations (normal center, counter flow center, counter flow side were experimentally varied and tested to determine the parameter combination that would produce optimal results. Images were processed using Pix4DMapper Professional and the resulting point clouds were optimized with scale and orientation constraints. The accuracies of the models were then verified through comparing actual-model road feature dimensions, TLS-model road width measurements and TLS-model road profile graphs. Actual-model measurement comparisons show accurate results as they agree at a centimeter level, having the largest error to be 2.5 cm among all dataset combinations. It was observed that the errors of the object dimensions decrease once the object dimensions are used as scale constraints while those not used as scales generally had increasing errors as new constraints were applied. TLS-model road width comparisons yielded minimal errors as well, reaching to only 1.29 cm. However, TLS-model road profile comparisons produced relatively larger errors, with 20 cm as the largest among all dataset combinations. It was observed that the largest errors occurred in the comparisons between elevation differences of the sidewalks and gutters. Only the dataset which included images capturing the outer and the middle portions of the road shows the best fit with the profiles produced by the TLS as it yielded the smallest errors, the largest being 7 cm, and was therefore concluded as the best dataset combination for road surface modelling using GoPro camera. Such is the case because only the images of the outer portions of the road were able to capture the deepest regions of the gutters that are not visible in the view of the images taken on the middle portions of the road.
