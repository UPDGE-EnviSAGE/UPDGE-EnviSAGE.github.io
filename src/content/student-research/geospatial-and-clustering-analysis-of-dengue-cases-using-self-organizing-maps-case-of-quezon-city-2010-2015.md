abstract: |-
  Dengue is the most rapidly spreading disease in the world with more than 30% of the world's population at risk of contracting dengue (Dengue, 2019). However, it has been considered as one of the neglected tropical disease with few joint and coordinating efforts from national and international scene (World Health Organization, 2012). Last 2016, more than 2.38 million cases were reported by the Region of the Americas reported, where more than 375,000 suspected cases of dengue were from Western Pacific Region, and more than half of the cases was reported by the Philippines (Dengue and Severe Dengue, 2019). Dengue virus inflicts significant health and economic burden to the Philippines (Edillo, 2015). Thus, it is important to improve the country's current schemes for dengue surveillance and response thru better understanding and knowledge on the development of dengue. In this research, geospatial and clustering analyses of dengue cases in Quezon City thru GIS and SOM were performed. Self-organizing map was explored as a new method in clustering analysis of dengue incidences. Two clusters were generated for each clustering method. After clustering the barangays, the coefficient of determination increased for most scenarios compared to the OLS regression of the ungrouped data. Common predictors among the different regression models are the informal settlements and very low residential areas. Based on the significant predictors identified and trend of the dengue cases, SOM produced more logical classification than the GIS Grouping Analysis. Although, SOM takes longer time compared to the ArcMap Grouping Analysis, SOM is easier and simpler to implement.
