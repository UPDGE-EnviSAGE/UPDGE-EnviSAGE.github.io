abstract: |-
  On-site coastal water quality monitoring is challenging due to its labor-intensive and costly nature. Remote sensing offers a cost-effective alternative that covers larger areas, aiding in the monitoring of Protected Areas in the Philippines. Siargao Island Protected Landscape and Seascapes faces significant issues with high fecal coliform bacteria concentrations in its coastal waters. Fecal coliform bacteria, found in the excrements of warm-blooded organisms, indicate water contamination. This study aims to estimate fecal coliform levels using surface reflectances, band ratios, band combinations, and spectral indices from Landsat 8 and Landsat 9 images through empirical and machine learning methods. Data were first cleaned to remove outliers. For the empirical model, four regression models were tested, selecting the surface reflectance value with the highest correlation coefficient with fecal coliform bacteria. SWIR 2 (Band 6) exhibited the highest correlation. A logarithmic regression model was used to map fecal coliform concentrations in the Municipal Waters of Dapa, Surigao del Norte. In the machine learning approach, Support Vector Regression (SVR) showed a lower mean absolute error compared to the Artificial Neural Network. The SVR model generated an equation used to map fecal coliform concentrations. These spatial distribution maps from empirical modeling and machine learning provide valuable insights into the municipal waters' condition.
