abstract: |-
  The Philippines relies heavily on round scad production for its economic and nutritional needs. However, the significant surge in their market prices due to the declining supply has rendered round scad less affordable. By integrating remote sensing techniques and geographic information system into fisheries management, the productive fishing grounds can be located for each Fisheries Management Area (FMA) in order to increase catch efficiency, understand round scad habitat preferences, aid in fish stock assessment, and promote sustainable fishing. Through the generalized additive model analysis, it was determined that sea surface temperature (SST), chlorophyll-a concentration (chl-a), and sea surface salinity (SSS) altogether provide a more accurate estimation of the round scad catch than using one or two explanatory variables. SST and chl-a showed a weak negative correlation with fish catch, while SSS exhibited a weak positive correlation. SSS displayed distinct seasonal patterns, while SST and chl-a exhibited uniform seasonal patterns across all the FMAs. With chl-a and SST found increasing in the second and third quarters and then decreasing in the first and fourth quarters, round scad catch could be expected to decrease and then increase, respectively, during these periods. Round scads are most likely to aggregate in areas where SST ranges from 28.0-30.5°C, chl-a ranges from 0.06-0.75 mg/m³, and SSS ranges from 33.6-34.5 PSU. The round scad yield maps produced from the seasonal fish catch regression models identified the optimal fishing grounds for the dry season of 2021. The study confirms that the relationship between the oceanographic parameters and round scad catch, as well as their spatio-temporal distributions, varies across all FMAs, highlighting the unique ecological conditions in each area that affect fish catch volumes. Therefore, the findings support an ecosystem-based approach to fisheries management and the implementation of FMA-specific policies and regulations.
