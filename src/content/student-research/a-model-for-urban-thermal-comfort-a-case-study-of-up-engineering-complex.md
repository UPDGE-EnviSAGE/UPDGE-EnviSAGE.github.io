abstract: |-
  In dense urban heat islands, increasing green areas and increasing the albedo of built-up materials are foreseen as a way of promoting urban comfort. For this research, both strategies can provide various benefits such as an increase in energy savings, offer comfort and mitigate environmental concerns including climate change. The National Engineering Complex served as the study area. The effects of vegetation on microclimate and urban comfort were evaluated and studied using ENVI-met. Satellite images, meteorological and thermal data were gathered and utilized using the ENVI-met. The scenarios were limited to the following actions: adding grass, adding trees, and alteration of materials relating to albedo. The application of different cases showed that increasing the albedo will significantly reduce the air temperature and thermal stress experienced in the area as compared to increasing evapotranspiration. The cases for this study are classified as Case 1 for the addition of trees; Case 2 for the addition of grass; Case 3 for adding grass in the roof of the building; Case 4 for the combination of Case 1,2 and 3; Case 5 for the alteration of the road materials; and Case 6 for the combination of all cases. The highest tallied reduction of air temperature is at most 2°C which occurred from 12:00 pm to 3:00 pm on April 18, 2017. Furthermore, all cases in receptor 3, 5, 6 and 7 exhibited a relatively constant trend of thermal stress from 8:00 am to 12:00 pm. Also, the simulated data of air temperature have a strong correlation with the surveyed data, the value of association, R, is 0.877. The validated models of the study were able to reproduce the mitigation strategies of thermal stress. Lastly, Case 5 produced the most significant air temperature and thermal stress reduction, thus, the most effective scenario.
