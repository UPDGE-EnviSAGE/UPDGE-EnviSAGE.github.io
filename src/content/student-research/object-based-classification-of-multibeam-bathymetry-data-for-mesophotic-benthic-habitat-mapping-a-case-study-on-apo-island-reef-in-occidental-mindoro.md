abstract: |-
  Marine spatial management is essential to ensure sustainable use of marine resources in our archipelagic waters. But as a requirement, mapping of its different benthic features must first be conducted. Many studies have been done on the country's shallow waters, however, minimal information is known about its mesophotic reefs which lie from 30-150 meters deep. Since the UP Marine Science Institute recently surveyed the mesophotic zones of Apo Reef for the Geophysical Coral Mapping Project, this study aims to utilize its multibeam bathymetry data and ground truth data for benthic habitat mapping. Apo Island Reef was selected as the study area because it contains 4 out of the 10 diving sites from which the photographs and videos were obtained. 14 bathymetric derivatives were generated from the area's digital surface model: Slope, Aspect, Northness, Eastness, Slope of Slope, Rugosity, Ruggedness, Curvature, Plan Curvature, Profile Curvature, Broad Bathymetric Position Index, Fine Bathymetric Position Index, Mean Depth, and Standard Deviation of Depth. Each derivative describes the area's terrain morphology and provides essential information for the delineation and characterization of benthic habitats. Then, three object-based classification algorithms: K- Nearest Neighbor (KNN), Support Vector Machine (SVM), and Random Forest (RF) Algorithm, were used to classify the layers into corals, rubbles, and sand. Results show that SVM obtained the highest overall accuracy (89.76%) and Kappa Index of Agreement (0.842). Meanwhile, KNN and RF achieved an overall accuracy of 82.55% and 85.57% and Kappa Index of Agreement of 0.725 and 0.775, respectively. The results of the three classification algorithms obtained a similarity percentage of at least 71% which means good agreement between predictions of the three classifiers. Based on the result of SVM classification, 82% of the study area were classified as corals, 11% were classified as sand, at the remaining 7% were classified as rubbles.
