abstract: |-
  Because the Philippines is inside the Pacific Ring of Fire, its topography is changing at a high rate. With the continuous changes in topography, Digital Elevation Models (DEMs) should be updated as they are used for numerous purposes and high accuracies are expected of them. Currently, methods for updating DEMs are costly, such as aerial photogrammetry, and tedious as establishment of ground control points are implemented. Other methods found from related literatures show the updated DEM is not georeferenced to the ground reference system or the method is fast but still costly as it involved aerial photogrammetry. This study aims to develop a methodology for updating existing DEMs to account for the terrain changes of a relatively small area through close-range photogrammetry without the use of control points and DEM matching by Least Z-Difference Algorithm. Close-range photogrammetry without establishing control points was used as the basis for generating the DEM since acquiring data is fast and low-cost. DEM Matching is needed since the generated DEM should be transformed to georeference it to its proper reference system. Methodology of the research revolves around acquisition of photos, generating the DEM from the photos, transforming the generated DEM through Least Z-Difference algorithm, and updating the existing DEM. Results show that the area of change was successfully incorporated to the updated DEM and the average error and root mean square error in elevation of the updated DEM is just 6.3 cm and 15 cm, respectively. As the final output, a fast and low cost method for updating DEMs was devised.
