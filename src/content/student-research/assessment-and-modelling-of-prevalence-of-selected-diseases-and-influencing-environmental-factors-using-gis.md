abstract: |-
  The Department of Health (DOH, 2004) data showed that the ten top causes of death in the country include diseases of the heart, diseases of the vascular system, pneumonia, tuberculosis and chronic lower respiratory diseases. Along with other diseases such as malignant neoplasm of the lungs, and cerebro-cardiovascular disease, occurrence of these diseases may be attributed to several factors including lifestyle of the patient as well as factors attributed by the environment. Following this trends in the disease prevalence attributed to the environment, this study aims to elucidate on the role of the environment on the prevalence of the different selected diseases by determining the environmental factors that can contribute to disease prevalence and estimate the strength of environmental factors to the prevalence of the diseases using regression modelling. The study is limited to the National Capital Region (NCR); being considered as the smallest region in terms of land area but the most populated. Temperature, air quality, road network, abundance of built-up and vegetation were the environmental factors used in the study. Regression modelling was done through the use of a statistical analysis software (XLSTAT) and results show evidence of these contributing factors to the prevalence of disease. The disease with the highest variability was determined based on the value of its adiusted R² The overall adjusted R² value ranges from 0.50-0.78, Malignant Neoplasm of the Lungs being the highest and Tuberculosis as the lowest. Malignant Neoplasm of the Lungs was determined to have the most number of explanatory variables, namely PM 10, CO emissions, road density, temperature, built-up, and vegetation. The remaining diseases share the same number of explanatory variable and it was evident that PM10 emission plays a greater role that other air quality parameters in the model Applicable to all diseases, air quality was determined to be the factor of highest influence; factors by the abundance of built-up and vegetation respectively based on their standardized coefficients. Temperature was considered of least importance. All explanatory variables have direct relationship to the predicted model except the abundance of vegetation. NOx and SOx had been removed as explanatory variable as it showed no significance in the model.
