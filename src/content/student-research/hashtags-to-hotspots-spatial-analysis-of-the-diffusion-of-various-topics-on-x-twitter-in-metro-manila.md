abstract: |-
  Social media has reshaped how people interact, becoming deeply embedded in everyday life. In the Philippines, social media is pivotal in information dissemination, with Filipinos spending the longest average time online daily and a significant portion of that time on social media, including X (Twitter). While existing studies have explored how online information spreads in the physical space, few adopt a topic-based comparative approach to examine how these may influence geographical spread patterns, especially in the local context. In this study, we investigated the spatial diffusion of four trending topics on X in Metro Manila by analyzing user activity, spatial dependence with respect to population and GDP, and spatial patterns using spatial and temporal analysis techniques. User activity analysis revealed distinct daily and hourly average patterns across the topics, but also shares expected similarities such as weekend peaks, lower weekday activity, higher activities during usual work break hours at noontime and evening, and early morning lulls. Spatial modeling showed weak spatial dependencies, with 35 of 48 cases favoring OLS. Moreover, it also revealed GDP and population as key localized tweet diffusion rate index (DRI) drivers, indicating their impacts are mostly limited to individual cities. Spatial analysis using Getis-Ord Gi* identified Manila, Quezon City, Mandaluyong, San Juan, Malabon, and Navotas as frequent hotspots. However, Local Moran’s I revealed that San Juan, Malabon, and Navotas have consistently Low-High outliers, while Manila, Mandaluyong, and Quezon City formed consistent High-High clusters across most months of all topics.
