abstract: |-
  The degradation and loss of benthic habitats present a critical challenge in marine conservation, necessitating the development of effective mapping methodologies to address this pressing environmental issue. This research focuses on mapping three key benthic habitat types, namely sand, seagrass, and corals, utilizing mid-spatial resolution PRISMA satellite imagery. To ensure precise mapping, the PRISMA image underwent Simple Radioactive Transfer Model (STM) water column correction, accounting for attenuation caused by the overlying water column. Subsequently, the corrected image was subjected to Linear Spectral Unmixing (LSU) analysis, enabling the extraction of percentage of endmember abundance for each benthic habitat type. The LSU approach provided valuable insights into the relative abundance of the targeted benthic habitat classes. The obtained endmember p-values, determined using the Wilcoxon-rank sum test, were 0.8007 for sand, 0.08849 for seagrass, and 0.5167 for corals with a 0.05 level of significance. These values indicate the suitability of LSU in classifying benthic covers. Furthermore, the statistical significance of the differences between the endmember values confirms the robustness and reliability of the classifying PRISMA imagery using the LSU approach compared to the classified Planetscope imagery using Random Forest approach, which yielded an accuracy of 87.33%. The findings of this study highlight the potential of utilizing PRISMA imagery in conjunction with STM water column correction and LSU techniques for accurate percent cover mapping of benthic habitats. The accurate identification and quantification of sand, seagrass, and corals provides resource managers and conservation practitioners with invaluable information for informed decision-making regarding habitat protection and restoration efforts.
