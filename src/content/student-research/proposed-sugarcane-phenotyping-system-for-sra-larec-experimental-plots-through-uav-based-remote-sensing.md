abstract: |-
  In agriculture, crop phenotyping involves the measurement and assessment of physical observable characteristics of crops which includes its physiological and biochemical properties. Crop phenotyping is important in research and crop breeding because it is the expression of the effects of genetic and environmental factors to crop production traits such as the yield potential. In measuring the phenotypic traits of crops, ground observation can be labor-intensive, time-consuming, and invasive to the crops. This was observed in the current procedure of ground-based field phenotyping employed by the Sugar Regulatory Administration - Luzon Agricultural Research and Extension Center (SRA-LAREC) to its experimental plots. This research aims to provide an alternative to the procedures employed by SRA-LAREC on ground measurements of selected phenotypic traits (including chlorophyll content, leaf area index (LAI), stomatal conductance, plant height, and canopy temperature) by proposing a sugarcane phenotyping system workflow through the use of UAVs equipped with multispectral and thermal sensors. This proposed system will automate the phenotyping procedures that they carry on the ground. In designing the system, the researchers first assessed the needs and conceptualized a proposed phenotyping system design for the experimental plots of SRA-LAREC in San Jose, Floridablanca, Pampanga based on the specifications and on the procedures currently implemented by the agency. The identified components of the system were UAV campaign, image pre-processing, image classification, determination of estimation models (by partial least squares and linear regression) and offline archiving. Offline archiving was included as a part of the phenotyping system but was recommended for further study. The feasibility of creating the phenotyping system from open sources tools and software was also investigated, starting from image pre-processing. For each step, the determination of the suitability of methods and variables were done by statistical methods. Multispectral vegetation indices was determined that can be used to estimate crop phenotype such as chlorophyll content, stomatal conductance and LAI. 17 VIs were initially determined for this study. While for plant height, it can be obtained from the DSM and the canopy temperature from the thermal index from the thermal sensor. In image pre-processing, the determination of suitable image pre-processing method was done. As of the moment, Pix4D (a commercial software) was determined to be more suitable for image pre-processing than OpenDroneMap (an open-source tool). Since Pix4D was the determined more suitable image pre-processing method, creating a fully open source phenotyping system was not feasible yet for this study. In image classification, the suitability of sugarcane canopy masking for each phenotypic trait was done. After this, the estimation models for each phenotypic trait was determined. The final variables determined for the estimation models are; (1) masked NDREI and GEMI for chlorophyll content, (2) unmasked TVI for stomatal conductance, (3) unmasked GNDVI and NAVI for LAI, (4) masked DSM - DTM for plant height and (5) unmasked thermal index for canopy temperature. These estimation models were used to compute the phenotypic traits. The researchers finally designed the final system for phenotyping of sugarcane crops using the identified methodologies and variables. This system will guide the agency from the UAV campaign to the compilation of output traits computed from the estimated models.
