abstract: |-
  The preservation of Cultural Heritage sites is an inherent responsibility of the citizens of a country. To this end, developments in digitization serve as a permanent way of preserving a site at its optimum state, and the creation of accurate 3D models serves as a repository of measurements for future reconstruction. In this study, the Fort Santiago Dungeons, a cultural heritage site in the Philippines, was 3D reconstructed using the GoSLAM ZEB Revo RT which is a handheld Lidar scanner used in Indoor Mapping, and Simultaneous Localization and Mapping Algorithm (SLAM). Of which, the generated Dense Point Cloud produced acceptable results in terms of colorization using the accessory ZEB-CAM, completeness, and measurement noise. This extended to qualitative evaluations of the Dense Point Clouds of different formats (LAS and PLY) and colorization. The RMSEs obtained against corresponding ground measurements were 0.734cm and 0.739cm for the Non-colorized, and 1.308cm and 1.312cm for Colorized, respectively. Likewise, the derived Mesh files gave similar results for the RMSE with the Non-colorized at 1.044cm (LAS) and 0.999cm (PLY); and with the Colorized at 1.339cm (LAS) and 1.326cm (PLY). Overall, the analysis showed that the Dense Point Cloud derived from PLY rendered a better 3D model - as the dungeon's interior was better represented. Moreover, even though there were differences in the RMSEs of the measurements, due to factors such as the number of points and the processing itself, all of them are still within the 5cm threshold to be considered useful in the event of a reconstruction.
