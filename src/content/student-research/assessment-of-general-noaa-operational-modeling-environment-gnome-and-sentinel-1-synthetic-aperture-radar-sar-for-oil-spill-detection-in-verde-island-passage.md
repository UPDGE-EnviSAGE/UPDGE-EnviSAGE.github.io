abstract: |-
  Verde Island Passage, known for its rich marine biodiversity, requires utmost protection due to its vulnerability to oil spills. Immediate response to oil spills is critical for effective mitigation efforts, with Sentinel-1 images playing a key role in real-time detection and monitoring of oil spills and trajectory modeling such as General NOAA Oil Modeling Environment (GNOME) is essential for predicting oil spill movement and planning response strategies. The study aimed to evaluate the agreement between the two methods and compare it to in-situ observations. Findings showed that the methods had a low agreement in terms of area yet each approach exhibited distinct strengths across various metrics. The trajectories of both methods agreed in terms of centroid skill scores. In addition, S1 performed better when it was only compared to the in situ field reports. Regardless, using both methods hand in hand is more effective for immediate response, especially when Sentinel-1 images are unavailable due to their revisit timing.
