abstract: |-
  Laguna Lake is the national and primary water source in the Philippines. It has always been historically, economically, and ecologically significant to the people living near it, however, as it lies at the center of urban development in Metro Manila, it suffers from water quality degradation due to industrial, household, and toxic pollution. To prevent further degradation, water quality monitoring is advised. Assessment of Laguna Lake's water quality by Laguna Lake Development Authority (LLDA) is limited to sampling only on a few number of monitoring stations established over the lake. Unfortunately, water quality sampling is not enough to assess the spatial variation of water quality considering it may change within hours. This research addresses this limitation by presenting a methodology on water quality assessment using remote sensing products, such as satellite images, that is optimal given the spatial extent of the lake. In this research, water quality parameters that have bio-optical properties, such as chlorophyll-a, suspended particulate matter (SPM), and dissolved organic matter (DOM), were estimated using Landsat 7 ETM+ images. Linear regression analyses were applied to the satellite image reflectances and water quality estimates obtained from BOMBER tool to create models for estimation of the said parameters. These estimates were first validated using LLDA's actual water quality measurements of the lake for the same year as the satellite images' year of acquisition, to determine whether a significant correlation exists. Best models obtained from ordinary least squares (OLS) regression consistently resulted with band 4 or near-infrared (NIR) band as an explanatory variable. With all other explanatory variables that attained low variance inflation factors (VIFs) indicating multicollinearity, as well as Koenker (BP) statistic indicating non-stationarity, the best models were extended to geographically weighted regression (GWR). For all the images, the models for SPM gave the strongest R² value followed by DOM then chlorophyll-a. As an additional output, integrating water quality data and satellite image reflectances from the months of December to April produced a model for dry season, with an R² = 0.71 for Chlorophyll-a, R² = 0.92 for SPM, and R² = 0.72 for DOM. The best model for each water quality parameter was then used to produce concentration maps of chlorophyll-a, SPM, and DOM.
