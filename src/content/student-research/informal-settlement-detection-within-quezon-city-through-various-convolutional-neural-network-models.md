abstract: |-
  Quezon City has seen a rise in informal settlements, with ~195,000 settlers reported in 2021. These settlements that are often located in hazardous or already-owned areas are difficult to delineate due to irregular development. Traditional surveying methods are inefficient for this irregular behavior, prompting the use of machine learning instead. This study evaluates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for detecting informal settlements from Sentinel-2 imagery (2016–2024) and examines changes in their spatial extent over time. Sixteen models were developed under three categories: Patch-based, GHS-based, and a hybrid CNN-RF approach that leverages transfer learning. Models were evaluated using training curves, confusion matrices, Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), and McNemar’s test. Results showed that CNN-RF-based models performed best due to their ensemble learning strategy and effective use of GLCM texture features. The top model, CNN-RF-4, was a CNN-RF hybrid using 8-band imagery and overlapping patch extraction. Using CNN-RF-4, annual maps revealed a 4.68% increase in informal settlements—from 10.63 km² in 2016 to 11.13 km² in 2024—with growth in areas like UP Campus and Pinyahan. This study highlights the value of an ML approach leveraging transfer learning for geospatial classification and supports datadriven urban planning aligned with Sustainable Development Goal 11.
