abstract: |-
  This study utilized remote sensing and GIS, along with OLS regression model, to analyze the relationship between turbidity, sea surface temperature, chlorophyll-a concentration and the abundance of Crown-of-Thorns Starfish (COTS). Water quality maps were created using GIS, satellite imagery, and in-situ data, and validated with field data. Regression models were then built using the maps and COTS data. The results showed that turbidity was a significant variable in COTS abundance during both wet and dry seasons. In the wet season, a positive correlation with an R2 of 0.75 was found between Normalized Difference Turbidity Index (NDTI) and COTS population, likely due to increased phytoplankton biomass, caused by rainfall, promoting the recruitment and population growth of the starfish. In the dry season, a negative correlation with an R? of 0.58 was observed, possibly due to high turbidity associated with inorganic sediments that hindered the growth of phytoplankton, therefore lessening the numbers of COTS. No significant linear relationship was found between COTS and sea surface temperature (SST) for both seasons. Similarly, no statistically significant results were found between COTS and Normalized Difference Chlorophyll Index (NDCI) for both seasons. Regression models were generated for wet and dry seasons. Results showed that Wet Season Multiple Regression Model and Dry Season Multiple Regression Model both with NDTI and SST as explanatory variables produced statistically significant results with the highest R? of 0.78 and 0.65 respectively. Validating the predictive capabilities of the models revealed that they were not accurate in predicting COTS numbers with mean absolute error of 391 COTS for the Wet Season Multiple Regression Model and 559 COTS for Dry Season Multiple Regression Model. Both models had a mean absolute percentage error of more than 100% indicating that predicted values are much larger than actual values. This is potentially due to the limited variables and a small dataset. Despite the poor prediction performance of the models, the study was able to provide an insight on the relationship of water quality parameters with the abundance of COTS. Recommendations for future research include collecting additional data, recording precise coordinates of COTS locations, and exploring more explanatory variables to improve the predictive capabilities of future models.
