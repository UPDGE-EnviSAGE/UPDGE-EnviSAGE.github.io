abstract: |-
  The most recent nationwide assessment of coral reefs in the Philippines showed a marked decline in the condition of the local reefs for the past decades. Hard coral cover of majority of the reefs across the country are in poor condition. Lack of coral reef monitoring for most areas can be viewed as one of the causes of this decline. This may be attributed to the traditional coral reef assessment methods being time-consuming and costly. The development of remote sensing for large scale coral reef mapping has been proven reliable and a cost-efficient alternative. With the rise of accessible and affordable satellite imagery such as PlanetScope, hard coral cover can be examined using four bands at 3-meter spatial resolution and daily temporal resolution image. This study investigates the ability of PlanetScope image for hard coral cover mapping using mixture tuned matched filtering technique. Four PlanetScope images (2016, 2017, and 2018) underwent preprocessing methods including sun glint correction, water correction, and Principal Component Analysis (PCA). The results show that classification accuracies fluctuated from 22% to 76% across all images with different preprocessing combination methods. Although the study was able to obtain an accuracy as high as 76% for one image, the study was not able to replicate this in the other images. PlanetScope images' low signal-to-noise ratio and image-to-image inconsistencies, as well as the uncertain water condition and quality of the study area, have been identified as the possible causes in producing inconsistent results. Regardless, PlanetScope still has the potential for coral reef monitoring and management that can be further utilized by future investigations and researches given its high spatio-temporal resolution.
