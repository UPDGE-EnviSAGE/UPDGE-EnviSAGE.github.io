abstract: |-
  Water quality monitoring is fundamental to managing water resources to ensure their quality and the early detection of emerging problems. To complement the conventional methods of assessing water quality, remote sensing methods were used because they are efficient in terms of cost, time, and labor requirements. The BFAR Region V monitors the water quality parameters of Lake Buhi. Due to the limitations of traditional monitoring methods, BFAR cannot fully characterize the water quality parameters, which can significantly help in understanding the patterns of water quality in the lake. Thus, this study aimed to characterize the spatial and temporal variability of turbidity in Lake Buhi in 2020 from Sentinel-2 images using Geographically Weighted Regression (GWR) and Normalized Difference Turbidity Index (NDTI). In the absence of sufficient in-situ turbidity data, the GWR-derived turbidity was used as an alternative. Results showed that GWR accurately estimated turbidity with results achieving coefficient of determination of up to R² = 0.98. GWR-derived turbidity were regressed with NDTI values to acquire turbidity models for dry and wet seasons. The best regression model for dry season and wet season achieved R² = 0.57 and R² = 0.22, respectively. During validation, the best turbidity model for the dry season achieved sufficient results with R² = 0.59, RMSE = 0.60, and MAE = 0.15. Meanwhile, the best turbidity model for wet season achieved relatively good results with R² = 0.49, RMSE = 1.22, and MAE = 0.25 during validation. These models were used to estimate turbidity for other months of 2020 and consequently, assess the spatial and temporal variability of turbidity in the lake. The analysis revealed that Lake Buhi is more turbid during the dry season than the wet season. Turbidity during the dry season is governed by its natural water flow, while its turbidity during the wet season is heavily influenced by precipitation.
