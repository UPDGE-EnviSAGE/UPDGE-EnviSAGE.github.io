abstract: |-
  Metro Manila, the Philippines’ largest urban agglomeration, exemplifies the spatial concentration of economic activity characteristic of agglomerated regions. While core cities enjoy strong connectivity and agglomeration benefits, peripheral areas continue to experience marginalization, highlighting persistent uneven development. This study investigates the evolving dynamics of economic development across Metro Manila from 2015 to 2020 using a multi-phase approach integrating spatial pattern analysis, social network analysis (SNA), and spatial modeling. Phase 1 uses nighttime light data as proxy for economic activity, revealing positive spatial autocorrelation with persistent economic hotspots concentrated in central areas and cold spots in peripheral zones, indicating gradual outward diffusion of growth. Phase 2 combines economic indicators, transportation metrics, and a gravity model to explore intercity economic connections, uncovering continued dominance by core cities such as Makati, Manila, Pasay, and Quezon City alongside limited integration of peripheral cities. This phase reveals shifting city roles, network fluctuations, and enduring spatial inequalities. Phase 3 applies spatial models to assess how a city’s network position influences economic performance, exposing competitive and collaborative metropolitan dynamics over time. Findings underscore the importance of enhancing physical connectivity, strengthening regional governance, and fostering “bridge” cities to promote balanced, urban development and reduce economic disparities.
