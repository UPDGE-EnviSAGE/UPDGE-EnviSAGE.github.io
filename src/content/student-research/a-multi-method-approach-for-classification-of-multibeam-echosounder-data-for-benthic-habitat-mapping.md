abstract: |-
  In line with the concerns for the conservation of marine ecosystems, Multibeam echosounder systems (MBES) are acknowledged as one of the effective method of mapping benthic habitats. A broad range of approaches have been formulated for the classification of features of the benthic sea floor although the qualitative characterization of these approaches is still underdeveloped. The objective of this research paper is to compare different approaches or classification techniques to qualitatively evaluate the acquired MBES data and provide accuracy for each method. The Manila Channel in Puerto Galera was surveyed using multibeam echosounder and the acquired data was cleaned and processed through Hypack. The derived backscatter and bathvmetric data was interpolated in ARCGIS using Too to Raster and Inverse Distance Weighted (IDW) and was assessed using classification techniques - Support Vector Machines (SVM), Classification and Regression Trees (DTC), and Supervised Classification. The generated model of interpolation showed that Top to Raster is more precise in yielding of hydrological Digital Elevation Model (DEM). Results from the qualitative and accuracy assessment demonstrates that Supervised Classification - Maximum Likelihood surpassed SVM and DTC, as this method produced a more accurate classification of the features in accordance to the manual validation of the data. The habitat maps generated by supervised classification provides a better analysis and characterization of the benthic sea floor.
