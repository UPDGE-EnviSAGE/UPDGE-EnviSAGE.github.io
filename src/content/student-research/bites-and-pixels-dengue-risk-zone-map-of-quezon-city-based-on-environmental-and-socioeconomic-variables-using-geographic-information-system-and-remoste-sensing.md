abstract: |-
  According to the World Health Organization (2022), the Philippines is included in more than 100 countries where dengue fever is endemic. Several dengue studies focused more on the clinical and entomological aspects of dengue, and a few research have been conducted to assess the spatial relationship between dengue fever cases, and environmental and socioeconomic variables using current advancements such as remote sensing (RS) and geographic information system (GIS. This study aims to assess the dengue risk of Quezon City during the wet and dry seasons from 2015 to 2020 by analyzing the environmental (i.e., land surface temperature, rainfall, humidity, vegetation cover, bodies of water, and water quality) and socioeconomic (i.e., land use, built-up area density, and width of streets) variables using R$ and GIS. By utilizing the Analytic Hierarchy Process, these variables relative influences were determined and used to generate dengue risk zone maps (DRZMs) per season. The DRZMs show that there is a high dengue risk in the northeastern portion of Quezon City. The DRZMs were validated using the number of reported dengue case data per barangay and the average dengue risk zone indexes per season. Pearson correlation coefficient, i.e. R = 0.515 (2015 wet season); 0.247 (2016 dry season); 0.254 (2016 wet season); 0.167 (2017 dry season); 0.455 (2017 wet season); 0.433 (2018 dry season); 0.215 (2018 wet season); 0.351 (2019 dry season); 0.177 (2019 wet season); 0.337 (2020 dry season), proves that the generated maps have a consistent positive linear association with dengue incidence. The utilization of these maps can offer invaluable insights to decision-makers, researchers, and health practitioners, enabling them to address the issue of dengue incidence in Quezon City effectively.
