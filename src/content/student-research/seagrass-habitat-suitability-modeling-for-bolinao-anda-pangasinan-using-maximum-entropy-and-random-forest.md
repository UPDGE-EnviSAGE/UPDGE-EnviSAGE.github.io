abstract: |-
  Seagrasses provide crucial coastal ecosystem services such as providing food and shelter to various animals, sequestering carbon, and stabilizing the sediments. Despite their ecological importance, seagrasses are facing rapid decline globally due to urban development, global climate change, and large-scale pollution. In particular, seagrasses at Bolinao-Anda, Pangasinan in the Philippines have recently declined due to increased anthropogenic activities such as aquaculture and coastal armoring. Given this, there is a need for systematic management and mapping of seagrass habitats to efficiently monitor and preserve these valuable ecosystems. This study focused on creating habitat suitability models visualizing the predicted suitability of seagrass habitats in Bolinao-Anda, Pangasinan. Through Pearson correlation analysis, highly correlated predictors were removed among 12 environmental parameters. The retained parameters included bathymetry, nitrate, phosphate, chlorophyll-a (chl-a), total suspended solids (TSS), temperature, and turbidity. The Maximum Entropy (MaxEnt) and Random Forest (RF) algorithms were utilized to develop a total of two seagrass habitat suitability models. Results showed that both MaxEnt and RF algorithms performed excellently in predicting the spatial distribution of seagrass habitats within the study area. The MaxEnt model performed better with an AUC of 0.951 compared to the RF model which obtained an AUC of 0.820. Bathymetry emerged as the most influential predictor for the MaxEnt model and turbidity was the least influential, while the opposite was the case for the RF model. Overall, the findings provide important insight on the use of GIS and machine learning to effectively predict seagrass habitat distribution in Philippine coastal waters.
