abstract: |-
  Existing direct measurements of forest aboveground biomass (AGB) or, in the case of this study, mangrove forests, such as forest inventory or direct measurements can be time-consuming, destructive, and expensive. As mangrove forests AGB has the highest carbon storage among the blue carbon ecosystems, it is important to measure or estimate its AGB in order to formulate management strategies and conservation plans for a more sustainable method of reducing carbon in the atmosphere. With the advancement of remote sensing technology, large-scale and highly accurate estimation of mangrove AGB became more possible. This study used three different satellite data (both optical and SAR): ALOS-2/PALSAR-2, Sentinel-1, and Sentinel-2. These datasets were subjected to Multivariable Regression (MVR), Support Vector Regression (SVR), Random Forest Regression (RFR), and Extreme Gradient Boosting Regression (XGBR) to determine what algorithm could provide better accuracy in estimating mangrove AGB in Zambales Province. Furthermore, individual and combined datasets were compared to identify the best predictors and models that would be reliable in estimating and mapping mangrove AGB in the province. Results showed that optimizing the models through feature importance selection improved the model accuracy. Specifically, the predictors from the optimized combination of the three satellites (VH_cort, VV_imcorr2, B8, CIG, VV _savg, VV, B1, HH_corr) produced the highest results using RFR with R? = 0.70 and RMSE = 22.80 Mg/ha. From this model, the mangrove AGB of the whole of Zambales was mapped, and discovered that the province has 34,539.61 Mg in total. Validation of the map produced was done by comparing GEDI-derived AGB and has shown no correlation between the two, suggesting limitations of GEDI.
