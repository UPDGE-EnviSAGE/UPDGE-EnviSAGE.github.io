abstract: |-
  The Philippines, being an archipelagic country, entails bathymetric mapping of its shallow waters. Traditional vessel-based bathymetry usually does not permit mapping of shallow waters since doing so damages both the equipment and the bottom surface. Hence, this research is conducted to test the potential of an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) in determining bathymetry of shallow portion of a beach defined to have depths less than 2 meters. Refraction correction algorithm was applied on the UAV-derived apparent bottom elevation to get the corrected bottom elevation of the shallow waters of a portion of Lian, Batangas. A corrector surface, obtained through least squares adjustment method, was also carried out to determine the best-fit plane. Depths extracted by subtracting bottom surface elevations from water surface elevations were utilized to generate bathymetric maps. Validation was executed by comparing the UAV-derived depths and the in-situ measurements of depth using total station. Root mean square error and standard deviation were calculated to assess the accuracy of the bathymetry derived from UAV. Upon refraction correction, the RMS computed for flights one and two were 45.186 centimeters and 26.884 centimeters while the standard deviations were 28.473 centimeters and 12.502 centimeters. With the aid of a corrector surface, the RMSE computed for the two flights were minimized to 6.367 centimeters and 5.989 centimeters while the standard deviation were reduced to 6.009 centimeters and 5.850 centimeters.
