abstract: |-
  This study aims to describe the relationship between dengue incidence and environmental variables (temperature, vegetation and precipitation) by defining their relationship to dengue incidence through Fuzzy Association Rule Mining. The researchers focused on dengue cases for municipalities in Luzon recorded weekly from January 2016 to December 2016 and used the first week of January 2017 for validation. Weekly incidences were incorporated with weekly temperature, vegetation and precipitation values through membership functions. These values were represented by fuzzy sets where each variable is given a value between O and 1, depending on its membership. Fuzzy sets spanning four weeks were used to generate records from which, datasets of different levels were derived. Support threshold was defined to preserve only those item datasets that have sufficient frequency of occurrence. Support count and confidence were calculated for rule creation and selection. Fuzzy support is the product of the membership values of the parameters over the number of records containing the dataset and fuzzy count is the quotient of the fuzzy support of the association rule over the fuzzy support of the antecedent. Higher- level item datasets were made until support count was no longer greater than the support threshold. Potential rules containing the dengue parameter were kept for further evaluation and were tested for their confidence. Setting the confidence to 0.95 resulted in the best association rule of if NDVI (1 week ago) is HIGH, Precipitation (1 week ago) is LOW, Precipitation (2 weeks ago) is LOW, then DENGUE outbreak is LOW. From the association rule, a prediction model was created. The prediction model created was compared to the actual number of cases in 2017 to check for its validity. The prediction model indicated that high NDVI and low precipitation have an accuracy of 86.71% in predicting low dengue incidence.
