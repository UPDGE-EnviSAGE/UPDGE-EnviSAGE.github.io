abstract: |-
  Air pollution in Baguio City has gotten worse throughout the years due to infrastructure, population, and vehicle congestion. Not only does it affect the city's economic and ecological development but also pose health hazards to its people. Particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter ≤ 2.5 m (PM2.5) is one of the most harmful air pollutants due to its tendency to penetrate in the lungs and cause various respiratory and cardiovascular diseases. With Baguio City's vision of becoming a world-class model city, air quality studies must be done in order to address the health risks of PM2.5 on its citizens. This research conducted a street-level estimation of PM2.5 concentration in Central Business District, Baguio City using temperature, relative humidity, pedestrian count, and vehicle count during a normal and an event-based condition (Panagbenga Festival). Air quality sampling was done during early morning (4:50am-6:30am), morning rush hour (6:30am-9:10am), and afternoon rush hour (3:40pm-5:40pm) periods on each condition using the CrowdSensor from the UP Institute of Chemistry. Linear and spatial regression analyses were done to determine relationships of the explanatory variables to PM2.5. Results showed that vehicle volume is positively correlated with PM2.5 level having a correlation coefficient = 0.666 and 0.6438, p-value = 0.012 and 0.4131, VIF = 1.841 and 3.01, and variable significance = 95.83% and 62.50% on normal and event-based conditions respectively. All other explanatory variables failed to show strong association with PM2.5 concentration. In general, PM2.5 concentrations were observed to decrease significantly during events due to absence of vehicles because of road closures. Recorded PM2.5 concentration average on normal and event-based conditions were 88.37 and 9.63, 58.92 and 12.84, and 46.63 and 60.56 µg/m³ during early morning, morning rush hour, and afternoon rush hour periods in that order. Accrediting only PM2.5 concentrations on normal conditions, Baguio City have exceeded the WHO limit of 25 µg/m³, hence, the city can be classified "unhealthy" in accordance with USEPA's air quality index.
