abstract: |-
  Land cover changes (LCC) pose impacts on water quality and hydrologic processes. LCC contributes to reduction of evapotranspiration and alteration of runoff volume and soil moisture. Meanwhile, river discharge (Q) transports chlorophyll-a (chl-a), nutrients, sediments, and pollutants from the adjacent land into the coastal area, promoting algal growth in nearby waters which may lead to the degradation of aquatic ecosystems. This study aims to determine the effects of LCC to Q and chl-a by simulating Q and performing correlation analysis between Q and chl-a concentrations. A SWAT model for the Agno River Basin (ARB) was set up, calibrated, and validated to model the basin's hydrology. Results of model calibration and validation showed satisfactory values of the four measures of accuracy considered in this study namely, NSE, R2, RSR, PBIAS: calibration (0.52, 0.59, 0.69, -1.9), validation (0.66, 0.71, 0.70, 3.7). Areas that are, in general, agricultural land, bare soil, shrubland, and forest were reduced whereas built-up and water areas increased. Seasonal variation of 9 was also observed, where wet seasons have the highest amount of discharge and dry seasons have the lowest, amounting to a 53% difference between the two seasons. River discharge for 2003 averaged 248.16 m³/s and 539.443 m³/s during dry and wet seasons, respectively, while for 2020 it was 255.377 m³/s and 550.437 m³/s, respectively. An increase in Q in 2020 as compared to 2003 was observed, amounting to 11.674 m³/s, 7.217 m³/s, and 10.995 m³/s, respectively for the whole period, dry, and wet seasons. Results also showed varying correlation between river discharge and chl-a concentration during different seasons: weak at 0.339 during dry seasons, and moderate at 0.449 and 0.412 for the wet seasons and for the whole study period (i.e., 2003 to 2020), respectively. With this, it can be concluded that river discharge does not significantly affect chl-a concentration in the ARB. The methodology presented in this study can be used to efficiently manage the river basin and mitigate the negative impacts of LCC. Finally, the results of this study can also be used for proper coastal management and land use planning within the ARB.
