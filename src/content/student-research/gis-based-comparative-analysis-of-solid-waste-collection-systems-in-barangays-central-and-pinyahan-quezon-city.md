abstract: |-
  Quezon City, one of the major cities for industry in the country, suffers from its projected fuel cost expenses in solid waste collection due to increased travel time for collection trips to farther relocations of landfills. An alternative bin-to-bin collection system as analyzed from other previous studies was proposed in order to determine if it can minimize time and fuel costs. In this research study, the current door-to-door system was compared with the proposed system by allocating bin locations and creating time-efficient collection routes by using Network Analyst tools in ArcGIS. Four door-to-door vs. bin-to-bin scenarios were compared: (1) using original household groups with bins mostly at intersections, (2) using regrouped household groups with bins mostly at intersections, (3) using original household groups with bins at road midpoints, and (4) using regrouped household groups with bins mostly at intersections. The resulting routes for all cases produced significant savings in travel time and fuel cost compared to the door-to-door system. Savings in both fuel cost and time for the bin-to-bin system using original household groups were higher than the case of regrouping the household groups. For the cases of choosing bin locations, locating at midpoints produced a 1% increase in savings in both time and fuel cost than when locating at intersections based on literature (ADB, 2016). It was determined that a bin-to-bin waste collection system using the original household groups with bin located preferably at midpoints of roads will be the most cost-efficient of all the scenarios mentioned, with 69% savings in fuel cost vs. door-to-door and 67% savings in travel time vs. door-to-door. Results suggest the inclusion of additional factors such as traffic, specific type of waste, and scheduling. Also, further studies can expound on the implementation of the proposed system.
