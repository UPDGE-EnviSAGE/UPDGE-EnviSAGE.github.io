abstract: |-
  Philippine forests have been in a steady decline for a long time, at one point it had reached a total land coverage of only 4% of the Philippine's total land area. As such, there is need for its preservation. The government has made attempts to counteract deforestation through the implementation of laws and policies to conserve forests. This study sought to assess the effectiveness of significant events such as the Oposa vs Factoran ruling and certain forest management laws implemented in the Philippines through the use of forest cover change analyses and statistical model analyses. The study area where these analyses were performed covered three protected areas in the Sierra Made region: the Casecan River Watershed Forest Reserve, Talaytay Watershed Forest Reserve, and the Northern Sierra Made Natural Park. The methodology of this study consisted of data preprocessing Landsat images from 1987 to 2010, which were then classified using Maximum Likelihood method which also came with accompanying rule images that had the probability values of pixels to be classified as certain classes as output. Pixels from the inside, outside, and at the boundary of the protected areas were selected, the probability values of these pixels were then used as input in ARIMA models which were analyzed, while the classified images were further processed to produce forest change maps. The ARIMA models were analyzed for patterns, with patterns being found among the outside, inside, and boundary points. The outside models showed a decreasing trend from 1987 to 1994, and then a rise afterward until 1998, decreasing again from 1998 to 2004, and then stagnating the rest of the way. The inside models were somewhat stagnant all throughout with slight changes from 1987 to 1995, 1996 to 2000, and 2001 to 2003. The boundary models showed decreasing trends from 1987 to 1993, then stagnated until 1999, before seeing a positive increase from 1999 to 2003, then a decreasing trend after 1993 and stagnation the rest of the way. From the analyses of the models and the trends established, the total time period was separated to 1987 to 1993, 1994 to 2001, and 2002 to 2010. The forest cover change analysis was then performed in the time periods specified. The ARIMA models and the forest cover change analysis were then compared with the occurrence and implementations of significant events discussed in the study. The analysis showed short-term success and long-term inefficiency in all the policies and events focused on. A recommendation brought about by this research is to perform it on a different area to cross-check the inferential conclusions made in this research.
