abstract: |-
  Weather phenomena such as typhoons and monsoons have been thought to cause coastal upwelling and subsequently increase phytoplankton (chlorophyll-a) levels in the upwelling area. Although the Philippines experiences such weather phenomena throughout the year, limited studies have been published regarding the occurrences and implications of upwelling on fishing activities and marine biodiversity in any part of the country. The province of Palawan stands out due to its seaboards serving as rich fishing grounds and its geography paralleling the winter monsoon, which prevails during November to February. Wind upwelling indices obtained from its western seaboard indicate wind conditions that favor upwelling for most of the time period specified. Sea surface temperature (SST) upwelling indices, on the other hand, occasionally indicate upwelling (UISST > 1oC) with most occurrences coinciding during the months where the wind conditions are the strongest. Correlational analysis conducted using Pearson coefficient reveal a weak to strong positive relationship (r = 0.05 to 0.65) between SST upwelling index and chlorophyll-a levels, a strong negative to strong positive relationship (r = -0.99 to 0.99) between SST upwelling index and boat density, and a strong negative to strong positive relationship (r = -0.70 to 0.60) between chlorophyll-a levels and boat density based on satellite-perceived nightlight emitted by fishing vessels.
