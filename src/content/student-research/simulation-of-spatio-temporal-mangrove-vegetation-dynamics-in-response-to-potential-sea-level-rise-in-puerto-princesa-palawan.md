abstract: |-
  Mangroves are an important part of a coastal ecosystem in terms of mitigating floods brought by storm surges but it suffers drastic impacts due to sea level rise (SLR). Puerto Princesa City, Palawan is home to 5,272 hectares of different mangrove species, however, studies show an alarming rate of annual sea level increase, resulting in significant mangrove loss. To visualize and estimate the possible consequences of SLR to the mangrove areas, specifically in Ulugan Bay and Oyster Bay, Honda Bay, and Puerto Princesa Bay, the Sea Level Affecting Marshes Model (SLAMM) software was used to predict the wetland changes in the three bays. The software was used to simulate SLR impacts at the years 2025, 2050, 2075, and 2100 for different scenarios. After running the software and modeling all study sites, years, and scenarios, the maps showed that mangrove shorelines of all bays are prone to inundation caused by SLR. Ulugan Bay and Oyster Bay, and Honda Bay will have minimal mangrove losses with 28.187 hectares and 99.915 hectares in 2100 under the worst SLR scenario. Both of these bays will successfully undergo landward migration in harsher SLR scenarios (RCP 4.5 and RCP 6.0). The results also show that mangroves in Puerto Princesa Bay are likely to be completely affected by 2100 as coastal squeeze may occur due to coastal developments. Despite the strict implementation of policies protecting the mangroves, SLR heightened by other factors such as anthropogenic activities and natural phenomenon put the mangrove areas at risk.
