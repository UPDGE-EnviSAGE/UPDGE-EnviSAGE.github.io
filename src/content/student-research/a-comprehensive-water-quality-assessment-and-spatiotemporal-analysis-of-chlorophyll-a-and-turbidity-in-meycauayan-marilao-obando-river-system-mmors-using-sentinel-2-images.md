abstract: |-
  The Meycauayan-Marilao-Obando River System (MMORS) is a 52-km complex river system which converges in the coastal waters of Manila Bay. MMORS supports multi-purpose uses such as fisheries, transportation, domestic use, and irrigation. However, due to extensive industrialization, it was listed in "The Dirty Thirty" by Pure Earth in 2007 as one of the 30 most polluted places globally. Traditional water quality assessments are often inefficient, prompting the need for more effective methods. This study addresses this gap by using remote sensing techniques for a comprehensive water quality assessment of MMORS, incorporating indices, seasonal trends, and hotspot analysis. Results showed that Water Quality Index (WQI) values in all 13 stations were beyond the maximum value defined for water bodies suitable for industrial and domestic use. The Normalized Difference Chlorophyll Index (NDCI) values were relatively higher during the rainy season (-0.316 to 0.348) but did not exceed the lower chl-a limit (7.5 mg/m3), suggesting that eutrophication might be a risk during the rainy season, potentially affecting aquatic ecosystems and fisheries. Meanwhile, the Normalized Difference Turbidity Index (NDTI) values were all mostly within the medium range of turbidity (-0.209 to 0.745) and relatively high in the dry season indicating that sedimentation and suspended particles are a concern, which can affect water clarity and quality. Trends indicated increasing levels of nitrates and turbidity, driven by natural water flow and precipitation. This study also reveals that NDCI and NDTI hot spots are located in the midstreams and upstreams of the river system near residential and industrial areas where continuous industrial discharges and urban runoff, highlighting the significant and ongoing impact of human activities on water quality in these areas.
