abstract: |-
  Seagrass ecosystems remain to be understudied and one of the least mapped benthic habitats. A number of studies have been made, but a knowledge gap exists for the comparison of water column correction methods for the mapping of seagrass cover using Random Forest (RF) classification and free and open data. To address this, the general objective of this study is to develop a methodology using Sentinel-2 (S-2) imagery for the mapping of seagrass cover in Santiago Island, Bolinao, with an emphasis on water column correction comparison. Preprocessing was done using resampling, sun glint correction, land-cloud-white cap masking, and water column corrections. Two water column correction methods were applied and compared, namely, Lyzenga’s Optical Model (LOM) and Stumpf’s Ratio Model (SRM). Spectral indices and Principal Components (PCs) were generated as additional inputs for the classification. RF was used for the classification into Dense Seagrass, Less Dense Seagrass, Water, and Sand/Rubble of the corrected imagery, the spectral indices, and the PCs. The use of SRM yielded a better, but comparable overall accuracy of 81% versus LOM’s 80.50%. Moreover, Dense Seagrass (84.7% User’s Accuracy, 61% Producer’s Accuracy) and Less Dense Seagrass (66.3% User’s Accuracy, 65% Producer’s Accuracy) are best classified using SRM.
