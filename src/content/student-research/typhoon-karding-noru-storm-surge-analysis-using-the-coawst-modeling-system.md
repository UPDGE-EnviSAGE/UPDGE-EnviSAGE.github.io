abstract: |-
  The Philippines is one of the most typhoon impacted countries in the world, and storm surge is one of the hazards typhoons bring. This study investigated the capabilities of the Coupled-Ocean-Atmosphere-Wave-Sediment Transport Modeling System (COAWST) modeling system in simulating the storm surge caused by Typhoon Karding in September 2022. Through COAWST, Regional Ocean Modeling System (ROMS) is used as the ocean model, Simulating WAves Nearshore as the wave model, and Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) is used as the atmospheric model. coupled using the Model Coupling Toolkit (MCT). Four setups were studied: I. ROMS only, i. ROMS-SWAN, in. ROMS-WRF, and iv. ROMS-SWAN-WRF, and four main variables were investigated: a. Surface air pressure, b. Wind speed, c. Free-surface, and d. Significant wave height. The coupled ROMS-WRF and the 3-way ROMS-SWAN-WRF setups were able to simulate Typhoon Karding's track with minimal positional error and accurately simulate the wind speed. However, the models overestimate the typhoon's minimum air pressure with p-biases of 7.74% (i and ii), 4.1% (iii), and 3.9% (iv), and with RMSE values of 68.529 hPa (i and ii), 36.744 hPa (iii), and 36.789 ha (iv). Moreover, the models underestimate the water levels with RMSE range of 0.31 to 0.35 meters & p-bias ranging from -72.56% to - 154.89% for the Baler validation point, while the RMS and p-bias range for the Real validation point are 0.30 to 0.34 meters, & -84.80% to -166.44%, respectively. Still, the models were able to simulate the storm surge and the rise in significant wave height at the Baler and Real points, in correspondence to the available recorded data, with setup iv being best in the simulation of storm surge. Overall, the COAWST modeling system may be used in simulating typhoons, and coupling can increase the accuracy of the results.
