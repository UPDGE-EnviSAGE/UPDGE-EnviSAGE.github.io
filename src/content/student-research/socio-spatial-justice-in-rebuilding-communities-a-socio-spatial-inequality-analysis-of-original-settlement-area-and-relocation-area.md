abstract: |-
  Rapid urbanization in Metro Manila has driven the growth of informal settlements, prompting government relocation programs aimed at improving the quality of life for Informal Settler Families (ISFs). While previous studies explored general urban quality of life and relocation program outcomes, limited attention has been given to how spatial reconfiguration through relocation affects resettled families. The recurring return of families to the city for work reveals a misalignment between relocation outcomes and actual needs. This study addresses that gap by developing a Socio-Spatial Quality (SSQ) index that integrates socio-economic and spatial indicators using the Analytic Hierarchy Process (AHP) and geospatial analysis. Indicators such as access to basic services, hazards, education, and employment were evaluated from both the National Housing Authority (NHA) and relocatee perspectives. Data was collected using government records, surveys, interviews, and focus group discussions. The SSQ values were validated and analyzed using spatial statistics such as Moran’s I, Spatial Gini, and ANOVA. Findings reveal that relocation to distant, off-city areas results in generally lower SSQ, with significant differences arising from the varying prioritization of indicators between the two perspectives. Spatial clustering further emphasized the role of geographic context in shaping outcomes. The study recommends participatory planning and spatially informed tools like the SSQ index to promote equitable, responsive, and sustainable relocation programs
